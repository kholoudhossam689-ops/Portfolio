The Problem

Discovery is scattered.
Planning feels like work.

Travelers — both locals and international visitors — don't always need another booking platform. They often need a clearer way to discover, understand, and decide.

🗺️

Too many scattered options

Travel information is spread across multiple sources with inconsistent quality, making it hard to confidently plan.

🔍

Hard to discover beyond the obvious

Famous tourist sites are easy to find. Hidden gems and less-documented destinations are not.

📍

Lack of destination context

Users need to understand what a place actually offers — its character, what's nearby, how long it's worth spending there.

📋

The gap between browsing and planning

Discovering an interesting destination and turning it into an organized trip are two different mental modes — most apps ignore this.

The Opportunity

One app, two types of travelers.

Safarni was designed to serve different travel behaviors without forcing every user into the same rigid path.

For Local Travelers

Discover new places within Egypt and build organized trips easily. Safarni makes it simple to go from "I want to go somewhere" to a structured itinerary in minutes.

For International Tourists

Discover Egyptian destinations visually and geographically. Understand what each place offers, how destinations relate to each other, and build a trip when ready — without pressure to commit up front.

Browsing doesn't require commitment.
Safarni can work as a pure discovery companion, or become a planning tool when the user is ready.

The Solution

A clear path from discovery to experience.

The entire Safarni product is organized around one idea: make the journey from casual exploration to a planned trip feel natural, not forced.

Discovery Experience

Where will you explore today?

The home screen immediately communicates Safarni's core purpose. A personalized greeting, a prominent search bar, destination category filters, and a featured destination card give users multiple ways to start exploring.

Visual-first destination cards make it easy to be drawn in by a place before knowing anything about it — the natural way people discover where they want to go.

Destination Exploration

Understand a place before you visit.

Each destination detail screen gives users a complete picture: a full-bleed hero photo, ratings, recommended visit duration, average temperature, a destination description, and a location map.

The "Start Planning" button appears only after all this context is provided — making the transition from exploration to commitment feel earned and natural, not rushed.

From Discovery to Planning

Ready to go? Set it up in seconds.

When a user decides to plan a trip, Safarni asks only the essential questions: When are you going? How many travelers? How many days? What's your budget style?

Three budget tiers — Budget, Moderate, and Luxury — give the planning engine a clear direction without overwhelming the user with pricing inputs. One tap on "Prepare My Itinerary" hands everything over to Safarni.

Smart Trip Planning

A day-by-day plan, without the spreadsheet.

The itinerary screen is where Safarni's value becomes most visible. Activities are organized into a clear chronological timeline — each with a time, activity type, title, and short description.

The day-tabs at the top let users move between days effortlessly. "Your Pick" badges highlight recommended activities, giving users a sense of personalization without decision overload.

Two action buttons at the bottom — "Refine" and "Plan Another Trip" — ensure the user always has a clear next step, whether they want to edit or start fresh.

Managing the Journey

Track every trip, in one place.

The My Trips screen organizes all travel into clear states — Upcoming, Ongoing, and Completed — with live trip cards that surface key details at a glance: destination, dates, and trip length.

The Profile screen keeps personal travel history visible and accessible. A concise stats row (Trips / Places / Memories) gives users a quick sense of their travel identity, while the Current Journey card surfaces active progress without navigating away.

Motion & Identity

The First Impression

The Safarni app opens with a cinematic splash screen. Using subtle motion, the app slowly reveals the depth of Egyptian architecture, setting a premium and immersive tone before the user even signs in.

This animation transitions seamlessly into the onboarding flow, maintaining the visual momentum and creating a sense of arrival rather than just opening an app.

Onboarding Experience

Your journey begins here.

The authentication screens immediately establish the visual identity of Safarni. A full-bleed hero photo of a Nile sunset with a sailing felucca communicates the product's essence before the user types a single character.

The sliding bottom sheet keeps forms clean and contained, layered naturally over the destination imagery — a design choice that reinforces the feeling of entering a travel experience, not just another sign-up form.

Design Decisions

Designing for discovery, not just transactions.

Building a product that serves two distinct user behaviors — casual browsing and structured planning — required deliberate decisions at every level of the UI.

Visual-First Discovery

Destinations lead with full-bleed photography. Users are drawn to places visually before reading any text, matching natural travel exploration behavior.

Clear Information Hierarchy

Each screen presents the most important information first — destination name, key facts, then detailed content — so users never feel lost or overwhelmed.

Consistent Card Patterns

Cards, badges, and action buttons follow a strict visual system throughout the app, making every screen immediately familiar regardless of where the user is.

Simple Navigation

Three-tab bottom navigation (Home, Trips, Profile) covers the full product scope without complexity. Users always know where they are and how to get back.

Discovery vs Planning Clarity

The product clearly separates browsing from committing. "Start Planning" only appears after the user has absorbed destination context — intentional friction to improve decision quality.

Itinerary Timeline Structure

The day-by-day timeline uses time stamps, color-coded activity dots, and card types to help users scan a full itinerary at a glance without reading every detail.

Reflection

What I Learned

Safarni challenged me to design for two distinct mindsets living inside the same product: the casual browser who just wants to explore, and the intentional planner who is ready to commit to a trip.

Balancing these two modes required thinking carefully about when to show friction, when to stay out of the user's way, and how to make the transition from exploration to planning feel completely natural.

Designing for Egypt as a destination also pushed me to create a visual language that feels warm, inviting, and culturally resonant — without turning the UI into a travel brochure.

Beyond design, I implemented the full mobile app interface using Flutter and Dart — translating every screen and interaction directly into working code. This end-to-end ownership of design and implementation gave me a much deeper appreciation for how design decisions affect real-world development constraints, and vice versa.
